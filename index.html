How random was it really?

Each reading becomes an 8-bit symbol (0 to 255) for how much it changed since the one before. Perfect noise fills the bars evenly.

Fairness test: 1,000 dice

Rolls 1,000 dice from the raw noise and 1,000 from the hashed seed. Raw shows the truth about your sensor. Hashed almost always looks perfect, which is why it can't prove anything on its own.

How does this work?

Collect: sensors are never perfectly still. Tiny jitters from heat and electronics show up in your phone's motion readings and in the quietest part of the microphone signal.

Changes, not readings: sensor signals drift slowly, so a reading is usually close to the one before it. The app measures the change between readings, which is close to independent, so it doesn't count the same randomness twice.

Measure: two checks in the style of NIST SP 800-90B. A frequency check (the more one symbol repeats, the less random) and a prediction check (how well each reading predicts the next, learning only from the past). The app keeps the lower score. These are estimates, not a certificate.

Health check: NIST's repetition count test rejects the noise if one value repeats too many times in a row, like a stuck sensor or a TV in the room.

Safety margin: only half of the measured randomness is credited, capped at 256 bits.

Extract and spend: the raw noise is hashed with SHA-256 into a 256-bit seed. Each result uses up credited bits, and no bit is reused.

Passwords: your physical seed is combined with the browser's own secure generator, so a password is at least as strong as the stronger of the two.
